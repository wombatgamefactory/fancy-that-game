Fancy That! — Complete Design Document
Overview
A 2–4 player gateway-weight tile placement and patisserie collection game.
Target complexity: Azul/Cascadia band (BGG ~1.8–2.3).
Target play time: 45–60 minutes.
Theme: Afternoon tea. Players are setting up a cake stand of fine British baking — finger sandwiches, scones, fancy slices, biscuits, tarts, and petits fours — for guests arriving for tea. The aesthetic is kawaii afternoon tea: charming, slightly whimsical, sophisticated rather than babyish. Think Beatrix Potter meets Sanrio meets the Great British Bake Off — refined, warm, distinctly British, with a dash of whimsy.
The name plays on the double meaning of "fancy":

A fancy = a small decorative cake (fondant fancy, French fancy, fairy cake)
Fancy (adjective) = elaborate, decorative, refined
To fancy (verb) = to like, to want, to take a liking to
A flight of fancy = whimsical imagination, delight
Fancy that! = British exclamation of pleased surprise

The title carries all of these simultaneously. When you claim a card, you've taken a fancy to a fancy. Fancy that!

Components
Tiles (100 total)

5 colours × 5 ingredient symbols = 25 unique tile types × 4 copies = 100 tiles
Each tile has: a solid colour background + a small etched black ingredient symbol
Colour = pattern matching axis (used for claiming patisserie cards)
Symbol = ingredient (used for track advancement when tile is TAKEN)
Held in a drawstring bag during play

The 5 tile colours (TBD — final names and palette pending):
The colours are abstract pattern markers, not literal pastry types. Five distinct, harmonious cozy-tea-coded colours that sit beautifully together on the table. Working candidates include soft pink, butter yellow, mint/sage green, dusty blue, and warm cream — but the final palette is open. The colours need not have an in-fiction meaning at all (Azul's coloured tiles are just colours).
The 5 ingredient symbols (locked in):

Strawberry — red, heart shape with leafy crown
Lemon — yellow, lemon slice viewed front-on showing segments
Chocolate — brown, square divided into a 2×2 grid of cubes
Almond — tan/cream, pointed teardrop with central seam
Caramel — golden, wrapped candy with twisted ends

Each symbol must work as both a black-and-white line drawing (etched onto tiles) AND a full colour symbol (for the cards' symbol strip and the scoring tracks). All five have been chosen for distinctive silhouettes that read instantly at 20mm tile size from across a table:

Strawberry: organic heart-with-crown
Lemon: round wheel-with-segments
Chocolate: geometric divided rectangle (flat)
Almond: pointed teardrop
Caramel: rectangular wrapped-candy with twist ends

Five completely distinct shape grammars — no silhouette confusion at distance.

Patisserie Cards

3 cards per patisserie type = 24 cards total
Each card shows: a 2×2 colour pattern (tile colours required), the patisserie illustration positioned within the 2×2, and a symbol strip along the bottom edge
All cards of the same patisserie type share the same colour pattern but differ in patisserie position (3 of the 4 possible positions used)
Symbol strip: 1–3 ingredient symbol instances (duplicates allowed) — these score at end game
Cards have NO flat VP — scoring comes entirely from symbols × track levels
Cards are assembled into a deck at setup, shuffled, and revealed one at a time as patisseries are claimed

Pattern types by patisserie (complexity → symbol count):
The pattern complexity ladder: 8 patisseries each with a distinctive 2×2 colour pattern requirement. Specific colour assignments per patisserie are TBD pending the final 5-colour palette and final 8-patisserie selection.
The complexity tiers:

1 pattern: 4× single colour → 1 symbol on card
3 patterns: 3+1 single-colour-dominant → 1 symbol on card each
3 patterns: 2+2 two-colour mixes → 2 symbols on card each
1 pattern: all four colours different → 3 symbols on card

Symbol strip composition (TBD per card):
Each card carries a primary featured ingredient (e.g. a Bakewell card features almond; a lemon-drizzle card features lemon) plus secondary ingredients that vary across the 3 cards of that patisserie. Final symbol assignments per individual card to be finalised in playtesting.
Personal Player Boards (4 total)

Dual-layer recessed boards, double-sided
Play area: 5×5 recessed grid (25 cells) — tiles slot into recesses satisfyingly
Border: Pre-printed coloured border around the perimeter — count toward 2×2 pattern matching
Scoring strip: Bottom row — 5 criteria, one per column (details TBD)
Symbol tracks: 5 tracks printed on board, one per ingredient, each with 5 steps (values: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5)
Player aid: Printed on board
Side A: Identical for all players — balanced border, simple scoring strip. For first play.
Side B: One of 4 asymmetric boards — unique border colour bias and unique scoring strip. Harder but more rewarding.

Each board will have a unique border colour distribution (biasing toward certain patisserie patterns) and a unique scoring strip rewarding strategies aligned with that setting.

Setup

Choose Side A or Side B of personal boards. Side B: players draft boards (pick one, pass left).
Place track markers at level 1 on all 5 ingredient tracks on each board.
Select which patisseries are playing: 2 cards per player (2p=4 patisseries, 3p=6 patisseries, 4p=8 patisseries).
For each selected patisserie, take all 3 cards of that type. Shuffle all selected cards together into one face-down patisserie deck. Place 3 figures of each selected patisserie near the deck.
Reveal cards from the deck one at a time, placing each face-up with 1 figure on it — reveal enough cards to have one per selected patisserie showing at start, or agree on N face-up cards simultaneously.
Dump all 100 tiles onto the market board. Collaboratively slide tiles into recesses. Takes ~20 seconds.
Randomly determine first player.


Turn Structure
On your turn, perform these steps in order:
Step 1 — Sweep

Point to any tile in the market board
Declare either its colour or its symbol
Choose one orthogonal direction (relative to your seated position: toward you, away, left, right)
Take that tile plus all consecutive matching tiles in that direction until you hit an empty cell or a non-matching tile
You always take at least 1 tile (the pointed tile itself)
Immediately, as each tile is taken: advance the track matching that tile's ingredient symbol by 1 step

Step 2 — Place Tiles

Immediately place all taken tiles onto your personal board
Placement rule: each tile must be placed orthogonally adjacent to either an existing tile on your board OR the board edge
Tiles from the same sweep do NOT need to be adjacent to each other — only each tile individually must satisfy the adjacency rule
All tiles taken must be placed this turn — no storage

Step 3 — Check for Patisserie Claims

Examine your personal board
If any 2×2 area (including pre-printed border tiles) exactly matches the colour pattern on any face-up patisserie card as read from your seated position (no rotation, no reflection), you claim it
Take the patisserie figure from that card and place it on the cell where the patisserie appeared on the card — covering that tile permanently (covered tiles no longer count toward future patterns)
Tuck the card under your board with the symbol strip visible along the bottom edge
Reveal the next card from the patisserie deck and place a new figure on it
You may claim multiple cards in one turn if multiple patterns complete simultaneously

Step 4 — Check Refill Trigger

Count empty lines on the market board (rows + columns)
If 3 or more lines are completely empty, the refill trigger fires:

You (the triggering player) may take any 2 tiles of your choice from those remaining on the board
You refill the market board by dumping tiles from the bag into recesses
Play continues with the next player taking the first sweep of the fresh market


If the bag is empty when trigger fires: take your 2 bonus tiles from remaining market tiles, do not refill — game end condition is now active


The Perspective Rule
Patisserie cards are never rotated or reflected. Each player reads every card from their own seated position. The same physical card presents a different 2×2 pattern requirement to each player. The patisserie figure therefore lands in a different tableau position for each player. Every player's cake stand is unique.
Example: A card with the patisserie in the top-right corner (as read by the player sitting south) places the figure in the top-right of that player's claimed 2×2. The player sitting north reads the same card with the figure in the bottom-left.

Scoring Flow (Critical — read carefully)
When you TAKE a tile:
→ That tile's ingredient track advances by 1 immediately
→ This is the only moment tracks advance — on taking, not placing
When you PLACE a tile:
→ Colour matters for pattern matching
→ Symbol has already done its job
When you CLAIM a patisserie card:
→ Place figure on board (covers one tile permanently)
→ Tuck card under board, symbol strip visible
→ No scoring happens yet
At END GAME:
→ Each ingredient symbol on your tucked cards scores VP = your current level on that ingredient's track
→ Track levels run 1–5 (starts at 1 so no ingredient ever scores 0)
→ Duplicate symbols on one card each score separately
→ Example: a Bakewell card with 2× Almond + 1× Strawberry, Almond track at level 4, Strawberry track at level 2 → scores (4 + 4 + 2) = 10 VP for that one card
There is no flat VP on patisserie cards. All patisserie scoring comes from ingredients × tracks.
Personal board scoring strip provides additional end-game VP (criteria TBD — one per column of the 5×5 play area).

End Game Condition
The game ends when the last patisserie figure is taken from the centre. All remaining players complete their current turn, then scoring begins.
Cards with all figures claimed are removed from the centre as they empty — the table visually simplifies as game end approaches. The dwindling figures are the game clock, visible to everyone at all times.
Total patisseries in play:

2 players: 4 types × 3 figures = 12 patisseries
3 players: 6 types × 3 figures = 18 patisseries
4 players: 8 types × 3 figures = 24 patisseries


Symbol Tracks — Detail
5 tracks, one per ingredient, printed on each personal board.
Markers start at level 1 at game start.
Each step advances by placing the marker one position right.
Maximum level: 5.
Track levels: 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5
(Starting at 1 ensures no ingredient ever scores 0 at end game)
Track advancement is passive and automatic — it fires every time you take a tile with that ingredient symbol, regardless of whether you declared that symbol during your sweep.

Key Design Principles (for reference when developing further)
Engelstein one-way flow: Tiles → board placement → track advancement → patisserie claims → end game scoring. Nothing feeds back upstream.
The central strategic tension: Colour sweeps build patterns (controlled colour, random ingredients). Ingredient sweeps advance tracks (controlled ingredient, mixed colours). A tile matching both your pattern need AND your ingredient need is a prize.
Non-mean interaction: You can never directly destroy another player's progress. Competition is over the market state (which tiles are available) and the patisserie cards (racing to build patterns first). No take-that mechanics.
Asymmetric boards: Side B boards create strategic identities through border colour bias and unique scoring strip criteria. Players who align their board choice with their patisserie strategy are rewarded.
The cake-stand effect: At game end, each player's 5×5 board is a personal afternoon-tea spread — coloured tiles surrounding kawaii patisserie figures in their natural positions. This visual end state is a core design goal. The game should look beautiful on the table throughout play and especially at scoring.
Cozy positioning: Sits in the cozy-tile-placement band alongside Calico, Cascadia, Harmonies — but on the British craft / kawaii / afternoon tea thematic axis rather than wildlife/habitats. Targets the same audience that buys Wingspan, Cascadia, and Calico, but with a fresh visual register.

Visual Design Notes
Symbol icon style (etched on tiles):

Bold solid black filled silhouette, not line drawing
No outlines, no shading, no internal detail beyond one or two strong negative-space cuts
Must read at 20mm size from 80cm viewing distance
Pictogram style — like a road sign or app icon
Five completely distinct shape grammars to avoid silhouette confusion

Symbol icon style (full colour, on cards and tracks):

Same shape language as etched version
Natural ingredient colours: red strawberry, yellow lemon, brown chocolate, tan/cream almond, golden caramel
Soft flat shading, minimal shadow, no heavy outlines — kawaii sensibility applied with restraint

Patisserie figures:

Iwako-eraser-inspired chunky kawaii sculpts
Each figure has a sweet little face / personality
Sized to slot satisfyingly onto board cells

Card art style:

Refined kawaii, not babyish
Charming patisserie illustrations with character
Soft palette, warm lighting, vintage tea-room sensibility
